John Cena recently discussed how WWE's creative process often triggered his rebellious nature during his career. Speaking on The Drew Barrymore Show, Cena clarified that he does not have an inherent problem with authority, but rather with the use of authority as a shield to avoid providing logical explanations for decisions.

> "So I have a problem with when there’s no good explanation. That’s my thing, right? I’m always in constant search for a good explanation."

Cena compared this to a parent using the phrase "because I said so" to a child. He explained that in WWE, creative directives were frequently delivered with that same dismissive mentality. Instead of accepting orders blindly, Cena would push back, seeking to understand who made the decision and the reasoning behind it.

> "In the creative space, especially, like, especially in the wrestling space, it used to be like, ‘Oh, because—because we said so.’ I just don’t stop there. Who’s ‘we’? Who do I have to talk to?"

Over time, Cena’s approach evolved from pure rebellion to a constructive search for clarity. He now values understanding the "why" behind a decision and is willing to change his position if a valid explanation is provided. However, he noted that throughout his career, he often encountered situations where no real explanation existed, leading him to feel taken advantage of.
