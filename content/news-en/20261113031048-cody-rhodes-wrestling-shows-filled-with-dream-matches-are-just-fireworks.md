Cody Rhodes believes that stacking a wrestling card with dream matches does not guarantee a high-quality show. According to Ringside News, the WWE superstar argues that combining big names in exciting matchups without a proper structure can actually diminish the audience's enjoyment, as a great event requires more than just a series of random bouts.

During an appearance on the What Do You Wanna Talk About? podcast, Rhodes discussed his philosophy while referencing Ethan Page's Intercontinental Championship victory. He highlighted the title's history as a workhorse championship, noting that legends like Bret Hart and Mr. Perfect could steal the show even outside the main event. He pointed to WrestleMania III as a prime example of a well-structured card, where both Hulk Hogan vs. Andre the Giant and Randy Savage vs. Ricky Steamboat succeeded as parts of a cohesive event.

Rhodes maintains that a wrestling card should be built like a single match, featuring a beginning, rising tension, and a grand finale. He stated:

> "It's not just random dream match after dream match because then you lose your interest. That's just fireworks."

He clarified that he is not against dream matches, but insists they must fit into a larger framework to keep fans invested throughout the entire night.
