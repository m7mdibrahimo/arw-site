Royce Keys and The 946 have brought plenty of destruction to WWE television, but setting The Bloodline vehicle on fire caused an unexpected concern for him. Speaking on HOT 97, Keys explained that he questioned the optics once he realized a group of Black wrestlers was going to film that scene in Birmingham, Alabama.

> "It's mayhem night. I was a little skeptical because we was in Birmingham, Alabama. Bunch of Black fools burning stuff in Alabama. I don't know. So uncomfortable."

Despite those initial doubts, he fully embraced the angle because the memories that stuck with him from growing up as a wrestling fan were the crazy moments around the matches rather than just the bouts themselves.

> "But it's one of those things that, for me growing up watching wrestling, it was all about moments. You know, you had matches, but it was the moments that made everything."

Keys added that fans frequently approach him in public to compare the chaos created by The 946 to the Attitude Era, which he regards as the best period in professional wrestling.

> "So, you know, throughout the day, whenever I'm out and about, I'm always having people, ‘Oh man, you're taking it back to the Attitude Era,’ which was the best era of professional wrestling. So just to even be mentioned with that, you know…"

For him, hearing those comparisons shows that ridiculous stunts like torching a car still hold a valuable place alongside great matches.
