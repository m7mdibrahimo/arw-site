John Cena says he no longer gets upset when people dismiss professional wrestling as fake. During a recent interview with Trevor Noah, the 17-time World Champion explained that such comments reflect the speaker's ignorance rather than the reality of the business. Cena noted that the industry’s shift from presenting itself as a legitimate sport to an entertainment product was a necessary reset that allowed WWE to go global.

Cena discussed how the grueling schedules of the 1980s made it difficult for performers to separate their personal lives from their on-screen personas. He cited Ric Flair and Hulk Hogan as examples of icons who struggled to step out of character. According to Cena, the transition to a PG rating and the rise of social media helped change public perception, making it acceptable for families to attend shows.

While the label still carries some baggage, Cena remains unfazed by critics. He stated: 
> "That comment right there, it doesn’t piss me off. It’s literally like an indicator of someone’s ignorance."

He credited Dwayne "The Rock" Johnson for breaking barriers for wrestlers in Hollywood, noting that the industry continues to evolve as more performers find success outside the ring.
