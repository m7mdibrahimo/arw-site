Tatum Paxley is unfazed by criticism regarding WWE's recruitment of athletes who lack a background in independent wrestling. While some fans believe wrestlers should pay their dues on the indie circuit before joining the company, Paxley maintains that the path taken to reach WWE is less important than the passion one develops for the business.

> "Yeah. I wouldn't say I have a chip on my shoulder. I understand why people—I don't understand why they have it out for me, you know? Because, to me, you really only get far if you have a genuine love for the business, you know? Because you can tell if somebody's not happy here, you know? And if you're not happy here, then, you know, you're welcome to go elsewhere."

Paxley, who transitioned to professional wrestling from a competitive powerlifting background, believes that the success of WWE's athlete recruits speaks for itself. She argues that those who thrive in NXT do so because they have cultivated a genuine love for wrestling, regardless of how they were originally scouted.

> "So, when you see, you know, these Superstars getting the success that they're getting, it's because they found the love for the business. And I think if you love something, you want to share that love with other people."

Ultimately, Paxley is content with her career and remains indifferent to those who remain upset about her unconventional entry into the industry.

> "So, I mean, people, you know, can get mad. It doesn't really bother me that much. I'm happy. I love what I do, and that's all that matters."
