Billy Gunn believes that modern wrestlers struggle to manage backstage conflicts effectively compared to his time in WWE. Reflecting on the drama during CM Punk's tenure in AEW, Gunn emphasized that wrestlers do not need to like each other to conduct business professionally. In an interview with Inside The Ropes, he credited Vince McMahon for maintaining control in the locker room, noting that while personal issues existed, most talent understood that business came first.

> "They do, but nothing like that. Like, Vince had things under control really good. Like, there’s this couple — like, we all — like, I don’t have to like you to wrestle with you or to work with you. I don’t have to like you. I don’t have to do anything. I just have to do business. And mostly, 99.9% of the guys always did that, right?"

When serious heat occurred, Gunn explained that it was handled privately by pulling parties into a room to resolve the matter behind closed doors. He applies this same approach as a coach, insisting that disputes remain between the individuals involved rather than becoming a public spectacle.

> "There’ll be that every speck now and then where you just pull them into a room, you know? You pull them into a room, you shut the door. So, you don’t do it out in public, like — and I don’t do it in front of the boys. It’s just like if I have something bad to say about coaching and I’m about to lose my — I don’t do it in front of people. I take them somewhere where nobody else can hear it because it’s nobody else’s business."

Gunn stated that the goal was always to reach a resolution before leaving the room so that the issue would not persist. He expressed disappointment that many wrestlers today seem unable to confront each other like adults, allowing drama to escalate instead of moving forward.

> "You pull them into a room, you shut the door, and you go, ‘All right, here we go. What are we doing?’ Like, if you got a problem, we need to fix it now. We’ll fix it now so when we walk out the door, we don’t have a problem anymore. Right?"

> "And that’s how grown-ass people deal with it. But nowadays, we don’t, and that’s sad. But there’s going to be people that are going to do that, you know."
