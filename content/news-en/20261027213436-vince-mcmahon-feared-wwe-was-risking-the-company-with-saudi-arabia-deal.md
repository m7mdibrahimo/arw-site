Vince McMahon had significant concerns about WWE’s business relationship with Saudi Arabia before the company finalized the deal. Former WWE executive George Barrios recently shared that McMahon worried the partnership could be “risking the company” following the disappearance of journalist Jamal Khashoggi. Barrios explained on My World with Jeff Jarrett that the relationship had initially grown from a simple event agreement into a broader strategic partnership for the Middle East, aligning with Saudi Arabia’s Vision 2030 initiative.

However, the situation became complicated after Khashoggi went missing. According to Barrios, WWE was just weeks away from signing the agreement when the incident occurred, leading to internal discussions about the potential reputational damage. Barrios recalled McMahon’s vulnerability during private meetings, stating,

> "I still remember him saying at one point — and I hadn't internalized this the way he had — he actually said, ‘I feel like we're risking the company.’ You know, he very seriously goes, ‘I feel like this is risking the company.’”

McMahon feared that proceeding could undo the company’s success at a time when WWE was performing exceptionally well. Despite these deep concerns and the difficulty of finding staff willing to travel to Riyadh to sign the deal, WWE ultimately decided to move forward with the partnership.
