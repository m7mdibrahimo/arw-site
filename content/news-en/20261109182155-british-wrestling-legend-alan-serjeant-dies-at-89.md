The wrestling world is mourning the loss of British wrestling legend Alan Serjeant, who has passed away at the age of 89. A two-time British Welterweight Champion, Serjeant spent more than 20 years in the ring and helped change how smaller wrestlers were perceived during the television boom of the 1960s and 1970s. According to Dave Meltzer of the Wrestling Observer, Serjeant passed away without a disclosed cause of death.

Serjeant built his reputation on technical ability, clever escapes, and impressive wrestling holds alongside names like Jim Breaks and Bert Royal. Before his wrestling career, he trained as an electrical engineer, served in the British Army as a physical training instructor, and worked for Ford before joining promoter Paul Lincoln in 1963. He joined Dale Martin Promotions in 1966 and made his television debut against Mick McManus in April 1966, launching a television career that included 52 televised appearances. He captured the British Welterweight Championship twice and competed at London's Royal Albert Hall, stepping away from the ring by the mid-1980s to work as a sports center manager and fitness coach.
