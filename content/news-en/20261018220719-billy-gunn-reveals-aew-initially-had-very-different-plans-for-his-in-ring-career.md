Billy Gunn was not originally expected to wrestle as much as he has for AEW. When he first signed with the promotion, his talent contract only included 12 wrestling dates for the entire year. Speaking to Inside The Ropes, Gunn explained that his role was primarily intended to be behind the scenes.

> "Yeah. So, my contract, I had 12 dates a year. Like, I really wasn’t going to wrestle, but I told Tony, ‘If you need me to, I’ll do some here and there.’”

He maintained both a talent contract and an employee contract, focusing his work on producing and coaching. However, the plan changed quickly once he began appearing on television. Gunn found himself working both AEW Dynamite and AEW Collision regularly, far exceeding the initial limit of 12 dates. He noted that he went from being used sparingly to working constantly, which was a significant departure from his original agreement with Tony Khan.
