Ken Anderson has shared his thoughts on what turns a performer into a great pro wrestler, emphasizing that the key element is establishing a genuine connection with the fans. During his career in the ring, Anderson competed for both WWE and TNA. In recent years, he has shifted his focus to training future talent, helping guide the development of stars such as Tiffany Stratton.

While speaking to Nick Teodoro at the Big Event, Anderson was asked what he believes makes a great pro wrestler. He explained that a wrestler must find a way to connect with the audience, though that path looks different for everyone. He recalled advice from Dr. Tom Prichard, who was instrumental in his early career. Anderson remembered asking Prichard what he needed to do to succeed, and Prichard told him that if anyone could bottle and sell that secret quality, they would, but nobody knows how to package it. Prichard added that while he could not define it in advance, he always recognized it the moment he saw it.

Anderson compared the dynamic to watching actors, noting how a background performer or someone in a small role can suddenly catch a viewer's eye and stand out. He stressed that the goal for a wrestler is to figure out how to be unique in a meaningful way, rather than just being different for the sake of it. He mentioned a conversation with D'Lo Brown about how promoters rarely focus solely on impressive move sets like a 450 splash. Instead, promoters and fans respond when a wrestler says or does something in those small moments that truly carries meaning.
