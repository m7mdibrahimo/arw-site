WWE superstar Cody Rhodes has taken aim at wrestling dirt-sheet coverage, accusing Fightful of repeatedly taking his comments out of context to generate harsh headlines. Rhodes made the remarks during a recent interview with WrestleTalk while addressing tribalism among professional wrestling fans.

According to Ringside News, Rhodes brought up how Fightful framed his previous comments regarding terms like "the Fed" and "New York." Rhodes clarified that he was not policing how fans talk, stating that fans can use whatever terms they prefer, but noted he finds it funny when independent wrestlers who have never worked for WWE use those phrases.

> "I remember I said it in just about wrestlers who say it, and the way Fightful tries to cancel me every week and the way they baited it was as if I said it to wrestling fans."

> "Wrestling fans, there’s no wrong way to be a fan. You can say ‘the Fed,’ you can say ‘New York.’"

> "I blink out when I meet an indie wrestler who says it to me because that’s, uh, okay, cool. All right. You haven’t wrestled that either. Uh, but that was more the joke I was making."

Rhodes expressed frustration with how casual interview conversations are converted into sensationalized stories. He noted that despite these efforts, the constant public scrutiny has only built up his resilience.

> "I feel bad for the producers because that happens every week. Fightful just, ‘Oh, we’ve got to make a headline about how Cody hates—has nine out of ten of his ideas are bad.’ This show is a fun show, a conversation, and their efforts to bring me down have only kind of made me, uh, I don’t know, I feel more bulletproof just ’cause, all right, I’ll ride through it, but go ahead."

> "I didn’t mean to derail with my rant. You were asking about tribalism."

When returning to the topic of tribalism, Rhodes discussed fan reactions surrounding the death of PAC. He cautioned that grief often leads people into petty arguments over who publicly posted tributes rather than uniting to honor the person who passed away.

> "I think something that I’d say is—and this is, I wish more people could understand this—is grief is difficult, and sometimes grief creates—you get mad about a thing that really distracts us and moves us away from the point. You start competing, ‘Well, he said something in tribute and she didn’t say something.’ None of that is as valuable as what you mentioned, the coming together over somebody."

According to Ringside News, Rhodes also revealed during the conversation that Kevin Owens set up a backstage camera at a WWE television taping. This allowed talent to record private video messages regarding Benjamin Satterley.

Addressing online criticism and fan hostility, Rhodes stated that he is currently in the prime of his career and feels more self-assured than ever. He explained that growing up in the wrestling business equipped him to handle public backlash.

> "When it comes to tribalism, though, on the other side of it, and companies versus companies or wrestlers versus wrestlers, I have now entered a point in my life and career where I have never been more confident in what I can do."

> "I’ve never felt more in my prime. I’ve never been able to be proud of myself but also laugh at myself at the same time with the things I do well and the things I do not so well."

> "So for me, if it helps you to chase me, if it helps you to make fun, if it helps you, I genuinely think there’s no wrong way to be a fan. Go for it. Go for it."

> "It doesn’t always feel right. It doesn’t always feel nice. Um, but if anyone can handle it, I was raised in the business to be able to handle it. I can handle it."
