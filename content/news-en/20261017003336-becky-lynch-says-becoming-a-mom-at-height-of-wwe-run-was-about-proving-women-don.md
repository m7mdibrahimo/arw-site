Becky Lynch was at the peak of her WWE career when she decided to start a family, a move she says was intentionally aimed at proving women do not have to sacrifice their professional dreams for motherhood. Speaking at the 2026 espnW: Women + Sports Summit, Lynch recalled how seeing Olympic sprinter Shelly-Ann Fraser-Pryce return to competition after having a child in 2019 inspired her. 

> "I will say this panel has a lot to answer for because in 2019 I was sitting on this panel and I was with this sprinter Shelly-Ann Fraser, and she was talking about how she had a two-year-old and she came back, and I was like, ‘Oh, it can be done. You can do that. You can go have a kid and come back and be at the top of your game.’ Six months later…"

Lynch, who was in the middle of a 399-day championship reign at the time, felt she had found the right partner in Seth Rollins. She rejected the traditional industry narrative that motherhood meant the end of a career. 

> "Well, you know, for me, I always wanted to have a family. I met the perfect partner. And, you know, for me, it didn't feel like I was giving up my top spot. It felt like I was doing something with my top spot because before it was, if you go off and you have a kid, well then that's it. Goodbye."

Lynch noted that top male wrestlers were never expected to choose between family and their careers, and she wanted to hold herself to that same standard. She believes this mindset has since become more common in the WWE locker room, as evidenced by the growing number of mothers in the company. Lynch successfully returned to the ring, won more titles, and solidified her point.
