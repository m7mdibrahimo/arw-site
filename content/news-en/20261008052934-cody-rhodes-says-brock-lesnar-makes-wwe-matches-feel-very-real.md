Cody Rhodes recently shared his perspective on the unique intensity of working with Brock Lesnar in WWE. During an appearance on the Flagrant podcast, Rhodes explained that Lesnar does not approach matches like typical performers, making the experience feel authentic and physically demanding. > “Brock is a unique animal to approach. He doesn’t even lock up. So how do you get in there with Brock? What do you do? Like, do you just run up and kick him? Do you run up and let him close? What do you do?” Rhodes noted that Lesnar forces his opponents to earn every moment of the match, describing the experience as the closest thing to a real fight. > “It’s very real. And when I say it’s very real, I’m not talking about getting punched in the eye or the nose.” He clarified that this is not about intentional harm, but rather a professional requirement to prove one's worth in the ring. Rhodes also recalled a story involving Solo Sikoa, who was once nervous backstage before a planned tag match that was never intended to happen. Solo, unaware of the creative plan, was prepared to ambush Lesnar simply to survive the opening of the match. This anecdote highlights the aura Lesnar maintains, as performers often perceive him as a genuine threat regardless of the script.
