WWE Hall of Famer Ric Flair and boxing legend Mike Tyson have secured a significant legal win outside the ring. A federal judge in Illinois denied a motion to dismiss a RICO lawsuit filed by the two sports icons and other plaintiffs in December regarding a cannabis company.

The lawsuit alleges that five individuals linked to Carma and Tyson 2.0 engaged in financial misconduct, costing the legends millions. The legal filing claims that two executives used company funds for personal luxuries, such as private jets, yachts, and home renovations, while others allegedly accepted undisclosed kickbacks.

The judge rejected the dismissal request from defendants Chad Bronstein, Adam Wilks, Nicole Cosby, James Case, and Christopher Volo, allowing the case to proceed. A representative for Tyson 2.0 expressed satisfaction with the court's ruling.

> "This ruling represents a significant development in our clients' pursuit of justice. Mike Tyson and his fellow plaintiffs have brought this case because they were victims of serious financial misconduct and a calculated betrayal of the obligations owed to them and their businesses."

The plaintiffs are seeking tens of millions of dollars in damages as the litigation continues.
