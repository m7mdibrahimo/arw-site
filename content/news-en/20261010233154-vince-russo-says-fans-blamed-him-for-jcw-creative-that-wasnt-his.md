Vince Russo has shared further details regarding his departure from JCW, highlighting a desire to protect his professional reputation. Speaking on the Two Man Power Trip podcast, Russo explained that he grew frustrated when fans criticized creative decisions that were not his own. Because the promotion was marketed as a "Vince Russo show," he felt that viewers automatically attributed every segment to him, even as Violent J took on a larger creative role.

> "As I get older, I need to be more protective of my name. The fact of the matter is, they were selling JCW as a Vince Russo show. They were trying to get a streaming deal because this was a Vince Russo show. But the more and more Violent J was injecting his ideas, it’s no longer a Vince Russo show."

Russo clarified that he holds no animosity toward Violent J, viewing it as natural for a promoter to want control over their own company. However, he reached a breaking point where he could no longer attach his name to a product that did not align with his creative vision. He noted that it was particularly difficult to read fan criticism for segments he had not written.

> "Always reading the comments that, ‘Oh, that's Russo.’ No, bro. There were a lot of things people were saying, and it was not Russo. It wasn’t me at all."

Russo added that such dynamics are common in wrestling, noting that promoters like Tony Khan, Billy Corgan, and Court Bauer often prefer to shape their own shows. Ultimately, these factors, combined with the stress he experienced, led to his decision to leave the promotion.
