JBL has pushed back against claims that former WWE Chairman Vince McMahon would have acted differently regarding WWE wrestlers performing in Saudi Arabia during the ongoing regional conflict. Critics have argued that McMahon would have prioritized talent safety over business obligations, a notion that the WWE Hall of Famer flatly rejected by recalling how McMahon sent talent directly into active war zones during the Iraq and Afghanistan wars in the 2000s. Speaking on Something to Wrestle, JBL pointed out that McMahon literally dispatched them to actual, legitimate war zones.

The regional conflict in Saudi Arabia prompted event organizers to move Crown Jewel to a different venue, and the show ultimately went off without incident, with all WWE stars and employees returning safely. However, reports indicated that some talent was hesitant to travel, with GUNTHER reportedly requesting to not be added to the match card due to safety concerns. His planned bout against Finn Balor was subsequently delayed.

Despite ongoing tensions and reported dissatisfaction among some executives within parent company TKO regarding the Saudi Arabian partnership, the relationship is set to continue. WWE is scheduled to return to the country next year for WrestleMania 43 in April.
