WWE veteran Nattie Neidhart recently shared her perspective on enhancement matches, noting that she does not enjoy the format. According to Wrestling Inc, the former SmackDown Women's Champion explained that these quick bouts, designed to give a featured star an easy victory over a less established opponent, often lack substance.

During an appearance on Busted Open Radio, Neidhart argued that wins are only meaningful when they come against significant competition. She noted that when the outcome of a match is predictable, it diminishes the value of the victory for the audience.

> "If you're going to win the match, you need to beat someone that means something. Otherwise, who cares if you won"

> "That's why I really don't like enhancement matches, and it's my own personal flavor of ice cream. I just don't like them, because enhancement matches, like, you know, sometimes you see a local/extra talent having a match with somebody, and you know, okay, we all know how this is going to go. So-and-so is going to beat them really fast, and get their hand raised. But if the person that you're wrestling doesn't mean something, your win doesn't mean something."

Neidhart contrasted these matches with high-profile bouts, such as Roman Reigns defending his World Heavyweight Title against LA Knight at Money in the Bank. She believes that working with top-tier talent like Reigns elevates everyone involved, regardless of the match result.

> "Roman's magic is that he helps elevate the person that he's working with. No matter what the outcome is, everybody is elevated. Everybody wins"
