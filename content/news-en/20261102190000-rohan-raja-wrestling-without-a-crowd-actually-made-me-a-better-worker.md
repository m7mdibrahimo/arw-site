Rohan Raja recently reflected on his time wrestling during the COVID-19 pandemic, explaining that performing without a crowd ultimately made him a better worker. Raja made his WWE debut on the April 29, 2021 episode of NXT UK, facing Teoman in front of no fans in attendance. Speaking on Fightful Overbooked's In The Weeds podcast, Raja admitted that the empty arena felt unusual at first as a babyface.

> "That was weird. I had my debut in front of no one, so my first match there in front of no one. I'm playing a babyface so when I first came in, the first few matches I'm like, ‘Yo, I have no crowd. Who am I going to feed the energy off?’ It was weird." Raja recalled that seeing Shawn Michaels, Matt Bloom, and William Regal watching the monitor near the curtain helped him lock in.

> "As soon as you’re walking through gorilla, you've got like Shawn Michaels and [Matt] Bloom and [William] Regal sitting there, so you have that instant like, ‘It’s go time.’ They're sitting there watching the screen like you're just about to walk out. But it was until I was waiting just behind the curtain, I could see the light of the camera and as soon as that turned red, I was in the zone." He noted that the lack of fans allowed him to focus better on pacing and structuring matches, noting that Joe Coffey shared a similar view.

> "I feel like though — and Joe Coffey said this as well — but I feel like wrestling in front of no crowd actually made me a better worker. You kind of have to place — when you're putting together the structure of the match and you're kind of — a lot of the times when you get to that next level, you can just change things instantly and restructure things on the fly. It just depends on what the crowd is feeling." However, Raja pointed out one major drawback of performing in the empty venue.

> "The biggest downside was there was no crowd, but the one downside was that everything hurt much more and that sucked. Every bump, I felt straight away. When there was no crowd and I'm slapping the mat, I'm like, ‘Yo, is this ring stiffer or something? What's happened?’ Then when I got to the back, I was like, ‘Yeah, I just felt stuff a lot more.’ I'm looking at everyone else and they're like, ‘Yeah, join the queue.’"
