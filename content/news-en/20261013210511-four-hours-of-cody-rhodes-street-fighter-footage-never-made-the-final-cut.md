Cody Rhodes' role in the new Street Fighter movie involved significantly more improvisation than originally planned. Co-star Andrew Schulz revealed that he, Rhodes, and Noah Centineo spent so much time ad-libbing scenes that production ended up with roughly four hours of unused footage. According to Schulz, the chemistry between the three actors encouraged director Kitao Sakurai and producer Cale Boyter to let them continue experimenting with different jokes and interactions on set.

> "It's this weird thing, 'cause I'm kind of trying to tease Cody, and then Cody's teasing Ken (Centineo), and then Ken is just caught in the middle of his mayhem. And credit to Kitao, the director, and Cale, the producer; they just let us shoot. That day was not supposed to be as long as it was, but we found something cool with the chemistry there." Rhodes noted that the improvisation continued until he eventually ran out of ideas, recalling a moment where he attempted to insult Centineo by calling him "Pee-Pee Hair," which caused the cast to break character.

> "I saw one; I think my bandwidth had been fully stretched. There's these really creative, clever ones [where] you're nailing it; you think you're killing it—but then at one point, I think I called Ken ‘Pee-Pee Hair.' That's where you had to know, ‘Hey, he's out of them.'" Rhodes also credited the Capcom representatives on set for ensuring that the improvised lines remained true to the Street Fighter characters. While it is unclear if the outtakes will ever be released, fans are hoping to see the material in future special features.
