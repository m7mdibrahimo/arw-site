Deonna Purrazzo shared her thoughts on the evolution of women's wrestling during an interview with The Ledgera magazine. While celebrating how common it has become for women to main-event shows and participate in major stipulation bouts, she pointed out that the industry still needs progress behind the scenes.

> "I'm most proud of how normal it has become to see women in stipulation matches and main-eventing television and pay-per-views. Twenty years ago, women often weren't given the time, investment or storylines necessary to warrant those opportunities. Now, we've proven over and over again that when the work warrants the spotlight, women can deliver in it. I've been fortunate enough to experience that progress firsthand. I main-evented three PPVs during my time in TNA/IMPACT and became the first-ever Iron Woman of IMPACT. I main-evented AEW Dynamite while under contract with TNA, defending the ROH Women's World Championship. In AEW, I was one half of the first-ever televised women's Texas Bullrope match. Last year, we saw the first women's Blood & Guts match, and in ROH, Athena has main-evented PPV after PPV. I've always believed women shouldn't be given the spotlight simply because we're women. We should be given it because the work we've done warrants it. What we're seeing now is evidence that women's wrestling draws fans' attention and deserves the same opportunities as the men because, every time we're given one, we continue to deliver."

Purrazzo stressed that wrestling still functions like a boys' club in decision-making areas. She expressed hope that the same success women achieved in the ring will soon spread to leadership and creative positions.

> "Where I think we still have work to do is behind the scenes. Wrestling can still feel like a boys' club, especially when it comes to positions of influence and decision-making. I'd like to see more women in creative, more women in talent relations, and more women working as agents and producers. We've proven that women belong in every position in front of the camera. Now, I'd like to see that same progress reflected in the people helping shape what happens behind it."
