Robert Stone recently reflected on balancing his professional wrestling career with his job as a high school teacher during his time in TNA, according to Pro Wrestling Illustrated. Wrestling under the ring name Robbie E during an in-ring career that featured several title victories, including an X-Division Championship win, Stone tried to resign from his teaching position after receiving a TNA contract offer in his second year. Even though the initial wrestling pay was lower than his teaching salary, he refused to pass up the opportunity.

To his surprise, the school administration urged him to stay and assured him they would accommodate his absences for live television broadcasts. Stone would wrestle live on Spike TV on Thursday nights, fly home on Friday morning, and head straight to school with hair gel still in his hair.

> "I finished school. I independent wrestled all throughout college. I got my degree. I got hired as a school teacher in a town over from where I grew up, high school phys ed and health, and then going into my second year of teaching, I had a tryout with TNA Wrestling and I got offered a contract. So, needless to say, at the time, the money was way less than what I was making teaching. But, you know, I had worked almost 10 years at this point in wrestling. I wasn't going to give up the opportunity. I attempted to resign from school teaching that second year. But they encouraged me to stay, and they said, 'whenever you can't be here because of wrestling, that's okay.' I was like, 'you're sure? Like, we have live TV every week. I'm going to miss a lot of days.' They encouraged me to stay, which was great because I was getting a teacher's salary at the same time as getting money for my first wrestling contract. So it was a great way to start being a full-time wrestler. But I would be on live TV on a Thursday night on TNA, fly home Friday morning, get someone to cover my first period and come into school the next day still with hair gel in my hair. I brought, I won the X Division championship and I brought it in. Like all the students would watch me on Thursday night and then see me in school on Friday. They were fist pumping in the hallways. It was a wild time because you know, I'm on Spike TV, live TV every Thursday, and then I'm in school during the week. It was like, so for them, it was cool. Like, think about it, your teacher, like we're watching them on TV every Thursday night. So it was very cool. But now, years later, yeah, once in a while, I have on Instagram or X, someone reaches out."

In the same interview, Stone expressed his belief that Kendal Grey and Kelani Jordan will headline WrestleMania in the future.
