Before joining AEW, MVP spent a significant portion of his youth incarcerated after being sentenced to prison at sixteen years old for armed robbery and kidnapping. He served nine and a half years of an eighteen-and-a-half-year sentence. It was during the final stages of his incarceration that he was approached with an offer to take steroids and wrestle for $100,000 a year, which sparked his interest in the industry. "I had never, ever, ever, ever had any dreams or ideas of becoming a pro wrestler," MVP said on the "Marking Out with MVP & Dwayne Swayze" podcast. "Never crossed my mind. Just randomly looking at these [guys], 'Yeah I'd become a pro wrestler. Yeah, I would do that. Sure.'" During his time at the West Palm Beach work release center, MVP began focusing on his physical transformation, utilizing creatine and heavy lifting to increase his weight from 205 to 245 pounds. Shortly after his release, he met a corrections officer who was also an independent wrestler, which helped him get his start in the business. Reflecting on his journey, MVP believes his time in prison was the catalyst for his career. "The truth of the matter is if I had never gone to prison, MVP would never have been born," he stated.
