The transition of WWE to high-definition television in January 2008 fundamentally transformed how wrestlers perform inside the ring. Speaking on the What Do You Wanna Talk About? podcast, Cody Rhodes explained that the shift required superstars to adopt a much more subtle approach to storytelling.

Before HD, performers had to rely on exaggerated gestures so fans in the cheapest seats could understand the action. Rhodes pointed to Hulk Hogan as a prime example of this larger-than-life style, recalling how Hogan used massive hand motions to explain complex match situations.

With modern HD production and advanced camera work, today's wrestlers can use smaller facial expressions and delicate movements. Rhodes noted that Roman Reigns is the best in the business at working individual cameras with his face, successfully balancing subtle television acting with the energy needed for live arena fans.
