WWE has encountered another hurdle in its ongoing efforts to secure trademark protection for Randy Orton’s famous “RKO” beyond merchandise. The U.S. Patent and Trademark Office refused WWE’s second attempt to register the letters for wrestling entertainment services in a nonfinal office action issued on November 3. The rejection stems from pre-existing registrations held by RKO Pictures, Inc., with the examining attorney noting that the studio owns six active trademarks, including identical typed registrations and rights for RKO Stage. WWE originally filed the application on July 6 to cover wrestling exhibitions across television, radio, and online platforms, alongside digital news services. However, the trademark office determined that these proposed services overlap too closely with the film, television, and multimedia productions already owned by the classic Hollywood studio, creating a likelihood of confusion. Additionally, the USPTO requested clarification on whether "RKO" holds specific significance or is considered a term of art within professional wrestling. While WWE already owns active RKO trademarks for merchandise such as t-shirts, this broader attempt faces the same roadblock that stalled its previous application. The refusal is nonfinal, giving WWE until February 3, 2027, to submit arguments and evidence, with the option to purchase an extension.
