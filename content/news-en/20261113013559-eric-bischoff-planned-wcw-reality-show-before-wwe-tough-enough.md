Former star Shannon Moore revealed that Eric Bischoff planned to launch a reality show in WCW years before WWE introduced Tough Enough. The concept involved housing 21 young wrestlers to film their daily lives and document their journey toward earning a spot on WCW Nitro, but the project never reached television.

During an appearance on Insight with Chris Van Vliet, Moore explained that he and several cruiserweights were hired in the late 1990s for the project, which drew inspiration from MTV's The Real World. Moore stated:

> "Still haven't confirmed exactly what it was that Eric Bischoff was trying to do, but from what it sounds like, he was trying to do Tough Enough before Tough Enough was ever a thing in WCW."

Moore noted the show was intended to capture the drama of 21 wrestlers living together. However, WCW released most of the group shortly after signing them, sending only a few survivors to the WCW Power Plant for training. Had Bischoff succeeded, WCW would have beaten WWE to the reality television format for wrestling.
