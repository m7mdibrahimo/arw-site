WWE Intercontinental Champion Chad Gable recently reflected on his run in AAA, which began when he performed as the original El Grande Americano character and concluded in May 2026 with a mask versus mask loss against the new El Grande Americano, portrayed by Ludwig Kaiser.

During an appearance on Chris Van Vliet's podcast, Gable explained how the experience provided him with deep personal insights. He discussed his lifelong tendency to seek approval from authority figures and coaches, a trait carried over from his amateur wrestling background. Gable noted that trying to satisfy the expectations of the Undertaker in AAA presented a significant challenge that ultimately prompted profound self-realization.

> "Sure. I’ve had coaches my whole life, whether it’s my high school coaches, Greco-Roman coaches, that I’ve always looked up to and wanted to appease, and I’ve wanted to make them happy and satisfied with my performance. That’s been a goal of mine. It’s just bred into me from amateur wrestling. It’s a trait that doesn’t always serve me well, but it’s just that’s part of me, and that was happening with Undertaker in AAA. I don’t feel like I was giving him what he was looking for the longest time down there, and it bothered the hell out of me. Deep down to my core, it taught me a lot about myself actually — self-realization about who I’m trying to please and why and all these things- but I made it a mission. I’m like, no, I’m gonna show him that I can give him what he’s looking for, and it was that night that I did the first brawl, the ringside brawl with the other El Grande, where he finally convinced me just let it go, just show me who you are."
