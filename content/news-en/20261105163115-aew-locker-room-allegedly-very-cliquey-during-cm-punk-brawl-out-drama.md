AEW faced a chaotic period during the CM Punk "Brawl Out" situation, and former WWE writer Robert Karpeles believes the company's internal issues ran deeper than fans realized. Speaking on the Two Man Power Trip of Wrestling podcast, Karpeles reflected on the turmoil surrounding the backstage brawl and the infamous press conference, admitting he grew concerned about the company's direction.

"He was very much a one-man shop, and you kind of need that. That was another thing where I was getting a little nervous with AEW for a little while, right around the time that the wheels fell off with all the CM Punk things, with the press conference that went south and the brawl and the reactions."

According to Karpeles, the locker room was divided into distinct cliques, with groups forming around Cody Rhodes, Kenny Omega, The Young Bucks, and Chris Jericho. He argued that these factions made it difficult for the company to move forward with a unified goal.

"It didn't feel decisive, and AEW felt very cliquey. There were different camps of people. You were either in Cody's group, or you were part of Kenny and The Young Bucks, or you were with Jericho and his crew, or you were everybody else."

Karpeles emphasized that when backstage drama escalates, leadership must step in to make firm decisions, regardless of popularity. He suggested that whether through respect or authority, the company needed a central figure to take control and resolve the conflict before it became public knowledge.
