WWE Hall of Famer Goldberg has no interest in entering politics, citing his wild past as a primary reason to avoid it. During an appearance on the BIFF podcast, Goldberg discussed his career and his commitment to charitable work, leading the host to ask if he had ever considered a political career. Goldberg quickly dismissed the idea, laughing as he explained that his younger years would likely create problems in the political arena.

> "Hell no. Hell no. For one reason. I mean, for a thousand reasons, but first and foremost, they look into my background. I I was a I was a crazy guy as a kid. You know, I I wasn't the responsible parent that I am now. But, you know, uh it's about evolution. And I had to do what I did as a kid to be the person that I am right now and to learn to appreciate every single thing that comes in front of me and not take advantage of of of anything."

Goldberg emphasized that while he does not regret his past, those experiences were essential to his personal growth. He stressed the importance of moving forward and taking responsibility for one's life rather than focusing on past mistakes. He also noted that he takes his role as a public figure and role model seriously, especially for his son, Gage.

> "As I said, my my job as a celebrity, as a ex-h Hall of Famer, a wrestler, an NFL player, I put myself on that pedestal for people to look up to and and I took that with pride and joy to be a leading force. Um, and that that is extremely important to me. Extremely important to me because I I I had a lot of good role models as a kid and and I now am a role model for a bunch of kids."

Despite his disciplined lifestyle, Goldberg admitted that he still has to be careful in certain situations because his intense personality remains. Ultimately, he believes he can make a positive impact on veterans and his community without seeking elected office.
