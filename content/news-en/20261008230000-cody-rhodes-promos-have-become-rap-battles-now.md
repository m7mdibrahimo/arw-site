Cody Rhodes recently discussed the evolution of wrestling promos during an appearance on the Flagrant podcast. While reviewing an old promo by JBL, Rhodes noted that modern segments have shifted toward a style resembling rap battles, where wrestlers focus on quick, witty insults. Rhodes said, "Promos have really become rap battles now. What do you got in your pocket, you know what I mean? You got these zingers on one another. Those are fun, but I feel like a really proper — a giving partner in those segments is the one that lets you say all the cool stuff. Let's you go, hey, I'll say this, I'll set you up. It’s going to be stiff, but you could say this. This is just a promo that's not cool, it's direct. Nothing cool about that. There wasn't a kind there like, ‘I want to be JBL.’" Rhodes also emphasized the importance of a wrestler having a genuine grievance when speaking, explaining that a villain is often most effective when they are speaking the truth in a harsh way. He noted that the process of delivering these promos is complex. During the interview, Rhodes also praised Dominik Mysterio and Solo Sikoa for their leadership roles within the locker room.
