John Cena recently shared that he likely had one or two years of wrestling left in him before deciding to retire. During an appearance on The Drew Barrymore Show, Cena explained that his decision was not based on a lack of passion, but rather a promise he made to himself not to linger past his prime for the sake of his own ego.

> "I want to be in the ring forever. I want to be there now. I was sitting here before you got here being like, ‘Man, this would be such an intimate building to have a show. I could put an 18-foot ring there. We would rock this place.’ Like, I love it. I’ll always love it. I also made a promise to myself to not do bad work, to not selfishly hang on for my own ego and just to feed my own thing and take away from everybody else."

Cena noted that he monitored his performance data, acknowledging that his body was becoming slower and weaker while recovery times increased. He realized that continuing to compete would only heighten his risk of a serious injury. Furthermore, his marriage influenced his perspective, as he now prioritizes responsible decision-making to ensure his long-term health with his partner.

> "When I’m on the roller coaster by myself and I got nobody else to worry about, if I blow out a knee, it doesn’t affect anybody... When I’m part of a team and we make a promise to each other... ‘I’m going to make responsible decisions so we have as many nights together as we can. We have as many mornings together as we can.’ So my priorities change, my values change, my beliefs change."

Ultimately, Cena views his retirement as a respectful act toward the wrestling business. He wanted to step away while still capable of delivering quality performances, allowing the next generation to take the spotlight rather than waiting for fans to notice a decline in his work.

> "I retired from wrestling and people will say, ‘He’s not passionate about the business. Look, he can still wrestle. Why isn’t he still here?’... It’s in my blood. Like, I’m passionate about the business. And for me, the most passionate act was to step away, give somebody else a chance because the business has given me a quarter century of opportunity."
