Michael Cole has discussed his future with WWE and revealed the age at which he hopes to retire from commentary. Speaking with Bobby Burack of OutKick, the veteran broadcaster explained that he recently signed a new multi-year contract and plans to continue calling matches for a few more years.

Cole, who is turning sixty, stated that he ideally wants to retire around the age of sixty-five. He emphasized that he has already spoken with WWE executives including Paul Levesque and Lee Fitting, making it clear that he wants them to be honest with him if his work ever begins to decline.

> So, I signed a multiyear contract a year ago, so I'm here for a little bit longer. I know some people will be happy about that. A lot of other people are going to be like, 'Oh my God, more years of Cole.' Listen, I'm 59, going to be 60 this week, actually. I want to get to retirement age. I'd like to get to 65 if I can.

Cole noted that he has seen many broadcasters stay in their roles past their prime and become shells of themselves, a situation he wants to avoid. He prefers to step away while fans still remember him performing at his best rather than wearing out his welcome behind the desk.

> But one of the things that I've been very adamant about, and I've talked to Paul Levesque about this, I've talked to my producers, my boss Lee Fitting, and I've made sure I want them to know that if they believe that I'm slipping at all, I want to know because I want to get out at the top of my game.

Despite thinking about the future, Cole confirmed he is not counting down the days and still genuinely enjoys his job across WWE programming. As long as he continues to have fun at ringside and during pre-show hosting duties, he has no immediate plans to walk away.

> So, I've got a few more years left in this. I still enjoy it. I love it. I'm looking forward to this weekend. There's so many different things I'll be involved in. I'll be hosting the Countdown show on Saturday. Obviously, I'll be at ringside calling all the action. When that red light goes on, it is still fun. And when it's not fun anymore, then it'll be time to hang things up.
